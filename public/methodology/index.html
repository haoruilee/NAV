Methodology

An estimate you can inspect.

The inputs, calculation and limits behind the numbers.

Estimated NAV = (Σ modeled holding values + configured cash) ÷ configured shares
Modeled holding = filing fair value × selected company valuation ÷ baseline company valuation

Start with the filing, not the headline

Each modeled company has a recorded holding fair value and, where available, a company valuation used as the filing baseline. If an eligible newer company valuation exists, the model multiplies the holding fair value by the ratio of the selected company valuation to the baseline company valuation. If no usable valuation or baseline exists, it keeps the holding fair value unchanged. This is a proportional proxy, not a security-by-security appraisal.

A transparent numerical example

Take an illustrative $40 million holding at the filing date, a $10 billion baseline company valuation and an eligible $12 billion company valuation. The ratio is 1.2 and the modeled holding becomes $48 million. That $8 million change is an input to the fund estimate. It is not an assertion that the fund sold the position for $48 million, that the same price applies to every share class, or that fund shareholders received an $8 million distribution.

Aggregate and divide

The implementation adds the modeled holding values and configured cash, then divides by the configured shares outstanding. It does not independently estimate every liability, fee accrual, dilution event or new transaction. Official NAV is normally a net-assets calculation; this proxy does not have a separate comprehensive liabilities line. A mismatch between the configured baseline and a newer reported NAV can therefore persist. The interface labels the result Estimated NAV and displays the reported reference separately.

Selection is not simply newest first

The existing selector ranks eligible records by audit trust and source class, then by event date within that ranking. A newer headline can lose to an older, more trusted record. Subject mismatches, combined-entity values and offering amounts incorrectly treated as company valuations are excluded by deterministic checks. Large unsupported jumps and old records without corroboration can also be blocked. The source page exposes retained and excluded records rather than silently deleting inconvenient evidence.

Two meanings of confidence

The estimate’s high, medium and low labels are based on the average age of selected company valuation events: below 30 days, below 90 days, and otherwise low. These labels are not probabilities of accuracy. The automated reviewer’s approval threshold is a separate gate for proposed source records. A strong primary source can be old; a recent article can be weak. The model cannot infer missing share-class rights, unknown holdings or future sale proceeds from a confidence label.

Disclosed coverage

Only positions with recorded weights and modeled inputs enter the corresponding coverage view. Zero-weight or unconfigured investments are not assigned invented weights. Unmapped exposure is not automatically classified as cash. See current fund coverage and dates.

External reference

SEC guidance on good-faith fair value determinations describes fund valuation responsibilities; our simplified estimate is not a replacement for that process.
