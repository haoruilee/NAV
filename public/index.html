ETF NAV & Tracking Method

How this site reads official NAV, market price, and private-company valuation evidence.

What NAV Means

NAV, or net asset value, is the fund's assets minus liabilities divided by shares outstanding. For listed closed-end funds, market price can trade above or below official NAV. For interval funds, official NAV is usually updated through fund filings rather than live exchange trading.

What This Site Tracks

The site separates official NAV from estimated NAV. Official NAV comes from fund filings and is treated as the baseline. Estimated NAV adjusts private holdings only when there is a usable valuation record for the portfolio company.

Valuation Method

Each holding starts from the fund's reported fair value. When a verified company valuation changes from the filing baseline, the holding is scaled by that valuation ratio. Cash and other fund assets stay at reported value.

Source Discipline

Claude Code reviews official company releases, SEC or regulatory filings, primary transaction announcements, and trusted media. Headlines about funding amount, revenue, talks, combined entities, or another company are automatically accepted or rejected before they can affect NAV.

Reading The Table

Verified means the last audit time, not the age of the valuation event. Click the Source badge for the underlying records, links, blocked items, and agent update log. This is not financial advice.
